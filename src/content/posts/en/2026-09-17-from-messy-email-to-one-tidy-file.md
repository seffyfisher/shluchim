I had an upcoming medical board, the kind you have to show up to completely prepared.
Armed with all the facts, all the paperwork, and a clear understanding of your rights.
At these boards you usually get only a few short minutes with the doctors,
and if you stammer or start fumbling for papers, you've missed your chance.

The problem is my life isn't filed away neatly in binders.
All the paperwork was buried in my email:
old visit summaries, referrals, PDFs attached with useless names like Scan_0041.pdf,
and loose email threads from a year ago.

Sitting down the night before a medical board to dig through Gmail hunting for documents
is a nerve-wracking nightmare.
Instead of clearing your head and feeling calm,
you're consumed by frustrating searches and panicking that you forgot something critical.

I didn't want another random folder on my computer to get lost in.
I wanted one tight, clear dossier I could open ten minutes before walking into the room,
and see exactly where things stand right in front of my eyes.


## How it worked:

Instead of creating a giant mess, I broke it down into three clear steps:

**1. A quiet extraction from my email.**
A shaliach pulled every relevant medical document directly out of my personal email,
without me having to manually search for a single file.
(Shluchim, singular shaliach, is Hebrew for "emissaries". It's what I call my AI agents.)

**2. Researching rights and official sources.**
A separate deep dive into the legal clauses and medical rights I'm entitled to by law.
This resulted in 11 well-organized files with clear source references.

**3. Spawning a dedicated shaliach for the mission.**
I asked the "creator of shluchim" shaliach to spawn a brand new shaliach,
dedicated purely to this single topic.

It's a focused, single-purpose shaliach.
Honestly, once this whole process is behind me, he can return to a place of pure grace,
but in the meantime, he can hold my hand and carry the whole case file.

## What came out:

One clean space, without cluttering the team lead's general chat.

Personal records and medical history on one side,
legal clauses and rights research on the other.
The new shaliach held the complete picture,
and walked through the critical points with me ahead of the board.

I walked into the room calm and focused,
without fumbling through files on my phone in front of the doctors,
and knew exactly what points to stand on.


## What to take from this:

**A narrow shaliach for a sensitive subject.**
A medical dossier doesn't belong in the team lead's general chat.
One shaliach, one context, less noise in your head.

**Split extraction from research.**
Personal email and general research don't run in the same pass.
Otherwise the personal facts leak into the general text.

**A shaliach can be temporary.**
Not every shaliach has to stay with you forever.
You spin one up for a specific mission, lean on him while you need him,
and once the job is done, you can bid him farewell.
