You install shluchim, connect your tools, and now what?
(Shluchim, singular shaliach, is Hebrew for "emissaries". It's what I call my AI agents.)

This is the exact wall everyone hits at first.
In theory, you have AI assistants that can handle almost anything.
In practice, you sink into the daily rush, forget they even exist,
and keep doing all the grunt work yourself.

The problem is they're completely passive.
They sit on the sidelines waiting.
And if you don't remember to initiate, open the chat, and come up with a prompt,
they just stand still.

I realized that for this to actually work,
the shaliach had to reach out to me first.


## How I made them proactive:

I set up a narrow, dedicated shaliach named "Work Now",
with a single mission: initiate.

Three times a day on weekdays, he pops in and asks:
"How can I help right now?",
and asks me to simply share or drop a screenshot of whatever is open in front of my eyes at that exact second.

And so it wouldn't feel like an annoying, rigid alarm clock,
I added a skill that makes it semi-random:
50% of the time he sends a message, and 50% of the time he skips it.

That coin toss makes all the difference.
It's no longer a bot pinging on the hour like a robot.
It feels like a teammate in the office glancing over, asking if you need a hand,
and not barging in when he isn't needed.


![An office door, a captured screen and a coin deciding whether to send a check-in.](/images/posts/thin-work-checkin-story-1672.webp)


## What changed in my head:

It flipped the entire dynamic.

Suddenly, right in the middle of a tedious task stuck in my throat,
a message pops up: "What are you working on right now? Drop me a screenshot".

And it shakes you awake in one second:
Right. I'm not doing this alone.
I have a team waiting for me to take things off my shoulders and pass them over.

I take one second, snap a screenshot of the spreadsheet, document, or open email,
send it without overthinking a sophisticated prompt,
and type a single short sentence.

From there, the shaliach takes the wheel and runs with it on his own.


## What to take from this:

**A shaliach needs to know how to knock on the door.**
When you're overwhelmed and stressed, you won't remember to delegate.
Build a shaliach that reminds you he's there.

**50/50 makes a robot feel human.**
Don't set rigid reminders at fixed hours.
A random element prevents messages from becoming background noise you tune out.

**A screenshot instead of a prompt.**
When a shaliach asks you to share your screen or an image of what's happening right now,
the barrier to entry vanishes.
One screenshot is worth ten minutes of prompting.
