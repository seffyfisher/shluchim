Until now, when I wanted a shaliach to have email,
I had to improvise.
(Shluchim, singular shaliach, is Hebrew for "emissaries". It's what I call my AI agents.)

An outside service like AgentMail,
or hooking up my own Gmail through a connector (a link between the bot and an outside app).
It worked, but it always felt like a band-aid.

Now Grok Bot has an inbox of its own.
A private address, on the mail.grokbot.com domain.

And honestly?
It works amazingly.

![The shluchim opening a giant envelope. One reads, the other is bored as usual.](/shluchim/images/posts/grokbot-email-illus-mailbox.webp)


## Step one, just ask:

I wrote to Basher, who runs my shluchim, asking him to open an inbox for us.
That's it.

And this is the part that's confusing at first.
I looked for a menu, I looked for settings.
There aren't any.
The change is in your head: you don't look for a button, you just ask the bot.

![My request to Basher to set up our email, and him starting to check which addresses are available.](/shluchim/images/posts/grokbot-email-ask.webp)

He asked what name I wanted,
checked that it was free,
and showed me an approval card.
Nothing was claimed until I approved.

![Basher letting me know he claimed an address for us, and explaining what it can do.](/shluchim/images/posts/grokbot-email-claimed.webp)

Worth knowing: the address is permanent.
You can't change it and you can't delete it, so pick the name carefully.

And it belongs to me, not to one shaliach.
All my shluchim can use it.


## Step two, sort the mail by topic:

Basher suggested a nice trick.
Adding a +tag to the address (a tag after the plus sign that marks which topic the email belongs to).

For example, name+invoices@mail.grokbot.com for invoices.
That way one address splits into several little inboxes.

He also suggested a few uses:
Invoices going straight to Hiba, the invoices shaliach.
Newsletter summaries.
Reports.
Keeping an eye on competitors.
And an address for feedback on this blog.


## Step three, the real test:

That morning I forwarded a sales report from work to the address, a PDF file.
I asked for it to be routed for analysis.

Then I asked Basher in the chat:
Did it arrive?
And who even gets this?

Basher found the email.
And then he told me something I hadn't thought of.

![Basher finding the email with the PDF, and explaining that nobody gets notified when mail arrives. (The chat was in Hebrew.)](/shluchim/images/posts/grokbot-email-found-mail.webp)

Nobody gets an automatic notification.
Incoming mail doesn't wake up any shaliach on its own.

It's also written in the official help.
There's no inbox view in the app, and no notifications for new mail.
For a bot to act on incoming mail, you need a routine (an automated task that runs when something happens).


## The fix:

First, Basher passed the PDF to Anali,
the analysis shaliach.

Then he built a routine.
Every email that comes in wakes Basher up.

Basher decides which shaliach handles it.

![Basher routing the envelopes, and each shaliach catching their own.](/shluchim/images/posts/grokbot-email-illus-routing.webp)

He doesn't reply or send anything without my approval.
And he updates me in one line.

And that's not just our rule.
According to the official help too, the bot sends email only when I asked,
and instructions that arrive inside an email don't count as approval.


## What people are doing with it:

I looked on X to see what others are doing with the inbox.

Someone shared that he asked the bot out loud "sign me up",
and the bot registered for an account on the MLH website with its own address.

Someone else just announced that from now on,
personal complaints go to his personal assistant at the bot's address 😄

And in the launch announcement, Grok Bot wrote that the bot can sign up for services,
reach out to businesses for you, and book meetings.

According to the official help, you can also collect receipts, newsletters and reports,
and trigger a routine only on emails from specific senders.

![Everyone around the inbox. Someone brings a letter, and the rest are already excited.](/shluchim/images/posts/grokbot-email-illus-inbox.webp)


## What to take from this:

**You open it with one request.**
Tell the bot "open an inbox for yourself",
and it does everything on its own, with your approval.

**Email doesn't wake anyone up on its own.**
Without a routine, the mail just waits.
So it's worth setting one up from day one.

**Nothing goes out without approval.**
The bot reads, sorts and updates.
It sends only when I said yes.

**From here, the sky's the limit.**
One email address became a front door for my whole team 🙂
