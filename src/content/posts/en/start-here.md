You open a chat, ask for something, get an answer.
Tomorrow you open another chat and explain everything from scratch.

After a month there's a heap of conversations you forgot ever happened.
And by the time I remember to ask for help, I've already done the work myself.

If that sounds familiar, this blog was written for you.

## What are shluchim:

Shluchim is what I call the permanent assistants I built in Grok Bot (singular: shaliach, Hebrew for emissary).
Each shaliach has a name, a role, and a job that keeps coming back to them.

On the blog I tell what they actually did, with my original requests.
Including what got stuck on the way.

## How to build your first shaliach, in six steps:

You don't need to understand anything to build a shaliach. You just need to know how to ask.

This is the short guide.
The whole story, with prices, limits and mistakes, is in the [long guide](https://shluchim.shinlamed.com/en/posts/grok-bot-guide/).

Haven't installed the app yet? The [official getting started guide](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/get-started) takes a few minutes. Computer or phone, both work.

### 1. Create a shaliach:

The app already creates a first shaliach for you, named Grok Bot.
Want one of your own? Click the + at the top of the sidebar (or Cmd/Ctrl+N), give it a name, and choose Create.
On the phone, the + is on the home screen, and that's where you choose New Bot.

![The Create New Bot window: a name for the shaliach, a choice of shape and color, and a Create button](/images/posts/start-here-step1-create.webp)

One job for one shaliach.
Not ten.
Say so in the first message:

> You're my shaliach for organizing my week. That's your only job for now. Remember that I like short answers, no detours.

![The first message to the shaliach, and its reply that it's on organizing the week and noted it down](/images/posts/start-here-step1.webp)

### 2. Talk to it, don't type:

The easiest way to start is with your voice.
On the computer, click Start voice input (or Cmd/Ctrl+D) and dictate.
On the phone, Start dictation is for dictating, and Start voice chat is for a live conversation.

![The message box in the app while dictating by voice, with the tip Click or hold Ctrl D to dictate](/images/posts/start-here-step2.webp)

You don't have to organize your thoughts first.
Throw everything at it, messy as it is, and it sorts it out.
Models cope with mess better than you'd think.

> Listen, I have three projects, a ton of emails, and I keep forgetting who I promised what. I'm going to tell you everything now, and you turn it into a list for me.

![A message dictated by voice, and the shaliach replies that it will organize things by project and by who I promised what](/images/posts/start-here-step2-reply.webp)

### 3. Give it one task:

One. Small. Safe.
A document that isn't sensitive and that you're allowed to share, and a request for a summary.
And never paste passwords into the chat. Ever.

> Here's a document. Summarize it in five lines: what was decided, and what's still open. Next to each point, write where in the document you saw it.

![A text document attached to the chat, a request for a five-line summary, and the shaliach's reply listing what was decided and what's still open](/images/posts/start-here-step3.webp)

### 4. Check the result:

Don't trust, check.
Open the document and compare.
Something missing or off? Show it where, and ask for a fix.

> In the second point you wrote that we decided to postpone. The document says we decided to approve. Fix it, and show me the sentence in the document.

That's also how you learn how far you can trust it, and with what.

![While checking the result, asking for the exact sentence from the document, and the shaliach quoting it word for word](/images/posts/start-here-step4.webp)

### 5. Set an approval rule:

The golden rule: before an email or a purchase, it asks.
Say it in words, in every task that touches the outside world:

> Prepare the email, but don't send it. Show me a draft and wait for my approval.

There's also a permanent rule, so you don't have to remember to say it.
Go to Settings (the account button at the bottom left, or Cmd+,), then the Permissions tab.
At the top, under Approvals, a dropdown shows Auto-review.
Below it, in the Always Ask section ("Stop and ask when an action matches these rules"), click Add Rule.
In the Applies to field, write in plain words what to stop, for example "send an email" or "buy something", and click Add rule.
On the phone, it's in Settings, in the Bot group.

When it stops, an approval card shows up, with Allow once, Always allow and Deny.
Always allow saves a permanent rule that approves from now on without asking.
So if you want to keep approving by hand, press Allow once.

One more thing: a credit card you saved in the Vault gets filled in at checkout without a separate approval.
Want to approve every payment? Don't save it there.

![Settings, Permissions tab: Approvals with Auto-review, and the Always Ask section with the rule "send an email" typed in the Applies to field](/images/posts/start-here-step5.webp)

### 6. Set a routine for 9:00:

When a task works, tell it to do the task on its own:

> Every day at 9:00, prepare a list of what's waiting for me today. Prepare it, don't send anything, and leave it for me here in the chat.

![The request for a 9:00 routine in the chat, and the shaliach replying that it set it up, with a Created routine line](/images/posts/start-here-step6.webp)

It creates a routine, and the routine runs in the cloud even when your computer is closed.
A new routine doesn't run the moment you create it.
If you set it up at 9:05, the first run is tomorrow at 9:00.

![Clay-style illustration: a shaliach holding a checklist with four ticks, next to a cup of coffee, a clock showing 9:00 and a closed laptop](/images/posts/start-here-02-1672.webp)

To make sure, click the shaliach's name at the top of the chat. In the Activity tab, under Upcoming, you'll see the routine.
Next to it you'll see when it runs, for example Every day around 9:00, and an on/off toggle.
The time follows the time zone in Settings, in the General tab, in its own section, in the Timezone row.

Tomorrow morning you open your computer, and the work is already there.

![The shaliach's side panel, Activity tab: under Upcoming, the routine with its time and an on/off toggle](/images/posts/start-here-step6-details.webp)

And don't open an office on day one.
Add another shaliach only when there's an area that keeps coming back again and again.
