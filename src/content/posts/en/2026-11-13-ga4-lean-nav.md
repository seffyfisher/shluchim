A one-second question: how many people visited the store today?
In GA4, it took me 15 minutes.

(Shluchim, singular shaliach, is Hebrew for "emissaries". It's what I call my AI agents.)

GA4 (Google Analytics 4, the tool that measures what happens on a website) is a powerful tool.
But its interface wasn't built for humans, to put it mildly.
Menus inside menus, and a screen that assumes you live inside the reports.

And we're not a small blog.
We're e-vrit, an Israeli store for ebooks and audiobooks.


## The example, one simple question:

The same questions come back every day.
How many users visited the store today?
How many this month?
And how many sessions (visits to the site) were there?


## Before:

I open GA4,
and land on Reports snapshot, a summary screen with a little of everything.

I look for the right report in the side menu.
And in a store, that menu is crowded.

On top of the regular traffic, there's a whole layer of commerce:
ecommerce (sales, carts, items),
purchases,
the checkout funnel (the steps on the way to payment),
and more events for every click on the way to the register.

Each one of them matters.
But when everything is open at the same level, nothing is really up front.

So I open a report. Not that one.
I open another. Not that one either.
And 15 minutes go on one number.

![Anali, a clay figure of a woman's head with round glasses and a messy bun, stares wearily at a GA4 screen crowded with overlapping windows, menus and reports.](/images/posts/2026-11-13-ga4-lean-nav-before-chaos-1672.webp)


## After:

I wrote a short prompt to Anali, my analytics shaliach.
No drowning in settings, and no clicking through ten menus.

I asked for a lean navigation in English, one clear collection,
with a key group up front.
And the rest folded. Not deleted, it just isn't in my face every morning.

In the same pass, I also added saved reports,
for the questions that always took time.
How many users visited today or this month, and how many sessions there were.

Now, when I open GA4, one collection is waiting in the side menu: E-vrit Ecommerce.
Inside it, Key Reports is open,
and All Other Reports is closed underneath.

The same question doesn't need a search anymore.
The reports are right there, at the top of the menu.

![The GA4 side menu of the e-vrit store: the E-vrit Ecommerce collection, with Key Reports open and listing User acquisition, Traffic acquisition, Referrals, Campaigns, Landing page, Events, E-commerce purchases, Transactions, Checkout journey, Purchase journey and Monetisation overview. Below it, All Other Reports is closed.](/images/posts/2026-11-13-ga4-lean-nav-real-nav-330.webp)


## What's waiting for me right at the entrance:

1. User acquisition, where new users came from.
That's how I know how many people came in, and what brought them the first time.

2. Traffic acquisition, sessions by source.
How many visits there were, and where they came from.

3. Referrals and Campaigns, sites that send people to us, and our campaigns.
In my view, these are the reports that answer "is it working or not?".

4. Landing page, the first page people land on.
Meaning, which page is actually the front door to the store.

5. Events, the actions people take on the site.
What they actually do, not what we hoped they'd do.

6. E-commerce purchases and Transactions, what was bought and which purchases happened.
The numbers a store exists for.

7. Checkout journey and Purchase journey, the steps on the way to payment.
This is where you see where people drop off before they pay.

8. Monetisation overview, a snapshot of the commerce side.
One look at the whole money side.

And whatever isn't on the list is in All Other Reports.
One click away, when I need it.


## A lighter version on the blog:

After the store, I did a similar version on the shluchim blog too.
There's no ecommerce and no checkout there, so the collection is even leaner.
One group called "Important", and under it a folded "More".

![Anali, the same clay figure, gives a small, satisfied smile next to a clean analytics screen with one chart and a few folded sections.](/images/posts/2026-11-13-ga4-lean-nav-after-tidy-1672.webp)


## What it gives us:

Until now, we opened GA4 and drowned in ecommerce and checkout buttons.
From now on, there's one front: what matters, and what's folded into "More".

And it's not just me.
The navigation changed for the whole company.

In a store, this isn't cosmetic.
It's the difference between checking a purchase funnel,
and getting lost inside the funnel's reports.

I'd love to hear if your GA4 also opens like a closet with no shelves :)
