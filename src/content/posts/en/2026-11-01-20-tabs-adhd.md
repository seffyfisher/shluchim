I've got 20 tabs open in my head,
and honestly I have no idea which one the YouTube is playing from.

I'm diagnosed ADHD (attention deficit hyperactivity disorder).
With a bonus of emotional regulation difficulty (managing how strong the feelings get).
And my kid is diagnosed too.

Meaning cognitive load (when the head tries to hold too many things at once),
with tabs that don't close.

So when I ran into this reel about ADHD and AI, it hit me.
(Shluchim, singular shaliach, is Hebrew for "emissaries". It's what I call my AI agents.)

![Seffy and his kid in matching Shinlamed caps, with colorful tab windows and screens piled behind them](/images/posts/20-tabs-adhd-kid-2048.webp)


## The reel:

[A reel (a short Instagram video) by Rivnitz](https://www.instagram.com/reel/DeerCj4m5UZ/).

He opens like this:

> So your brain has 20 tabs open, and they call it a disorder.

Then he drops this:

> AI lets the one with 20 tabs pick an agent for each tab.
> So for the first time in history, his brain is the right brain.

(Both quotes are from the reel.)

At my place, that kind of agent is called a shaliach.
And here I'm fully with him.

Only it sounds nice in a reel.
At my place it looks like this:

![A frame from the ADHD 20-tabs reel](/images/posts/20-tabs-adhd-reel-1206.webp)


## In reality:

On mobile I had over 130 tabs open for months.
Even with auto-archive.
And more in the work browser, and more in the personal browser.

That's the cognitive load of ADHD on a screen:
links and references saved "to come back to" that never came back,
jamming the phone, and the head too.

I sent them to 'Asaf', a shaliach that collects links and references.
Basically a brain dump (throwing out what's stuck in your head), for managing ADHD.
I just sent it, and that was it.

Tip: want to copy all the open links in the browser?
Type this in the address bar: `chrome://inspect/#pages`
(the list of open pages in Chrome).
And on mobile just go to the open tabs and do select all > share

He sent me back a tidy file, a links research hub.

And that's exactly what the reel was talking about:
every tab now has someone moving it forward,
instead of everything staying in the head and open.

From now on it isn't a tab that lives forever.
Every matter has a shaliach,
and the tabs themselves have a dedicated shaliach.
Personal, work, and mobile, in the same pipe.

![Chat with Asaf after sending the open tabs](/images/posts/20-tabs-adhd-asaf-chat-969.webp)


## Why we're the ones who are ready:

People talk a lot now about AI readiness (how ready we are to work with AI).
In my view, a head with ADHD has been training for working with artificial intelligence its whole life, without knowing.

1. **Several threads in parallel is the default.**
Managing several shluchim is roughly what the head already does.
Only now someone is actually working in every tab I open.

2. **We already get everything out of our heads anyway.**
Whoever doesn't trust their memory builds systems.
A shaliach is a system that also answers back.

3. **Asking for help isn't a failure.**
With a shaliach, that's simply how the work works.


## But let's not get carried away:

A shaliach for every tab is also a great recipe for opening more tabs.
Every answer from AI opens a new question.

And the rabbit hole (when you go into one topic and come out hours later somewhere else entirely),
gets deeper and faster.

Ten open threads, and none of them closed.
The shluchim work fast, but the one who decides what's done is still me.

Meaning the shluchim don't cure ADHD.
They amplify it, in both directions.


## About the pills:

Here I'm already not with him.

The reel says it isn't a disorder at all,
and digs at Ritalin and Adderall (medications for attention treatment).
At the end he suggests writing prescriptions for the people who can't imagine a different system.

A good provocation for a one-minute reel.
But it's a provocation, not a medical fact.

I'm not a doctor.
Treatment, with pills or without, is a personal decision you make with professionals.
And definitely when it's about a kid.
And for me it wouldn't have been relevant without it.

And ADHD is also emotional regulation, and days that are simply hard.
So "the right brain for the era" is not a reason to give up on help,
even if it matches the era.


## And for my kid:

So what is AI readiness for him, when there's ADHD at home?
In my view it's less knowing how to write a prompt (the instruction you give AI), and more:

1. Knowing what you want, before you ask.
2. Breaking a big task into the smallest pieces possible.
Exactly what is hard for us, and what a shaliach needs in order to work well.
3. Checking what came back, because a shaliach gets it wrong, and sometimes with full confidence.
4. Knowing how to close a tab, actually close it.


What matters most to me is that he doesn't grow up feeling his brain is "not okay".
And also not feeling he's "the right brain" for the era, and everything will work itself out.
