I was explaining to my mom how AI could fix the mess of timesheets.
And then I remembered who forgets to fill them in every month.

(Shluchim, singular shaliach, is Hebrew for "emissaries". It's what I call my AI agents.)

My mom works in HR.
We talked about the headache of collecting hours from employees.
Everyone has their own way of reporting, they send it by email,
and the whole month gets scattered across messages that need to be collected and completed.

I started explaining to her how much of this work can already be done automatically.
And then I stopped. Who am I to talk?
At the end of every month, I get an email from HR: you forgot to fill in your hours.

I'm the very problem she's describing.

**So I took out my phone**

I wrote to Basher, the shaliach who manages my shluchim for me:

> Go into the hours management system and complete the reports for the month I forgot to fill in.

I didn't want to reach the last day under pressure again, with empty days and emails from HR.
I also didn't want another reminder that I'd ignore.
I wanted the hours to actually get filled in, by my rules, before the month closes.

That was on October 30.
The shaliach found the empty days, filled them in as work from home with random hours, and saved.
He didn't touch the reports that were already in the system,
and he left the special days for me to decide on.

![The same report after the shaliach: the empty days filled in as "work from home" with clock-in and clock-out times, and election day marked separately.](/images/posts/2026-11-13-shaliach-punches-the-clock-hours-after-929.webp)

He suggested, I approved:

> Skip election day, no need to fill it in. Save the rest.

![Basher, the shaliach with the red mohawk, holds a paper card in his mouth and punches it in an old mechanical time clock.](/images/posts/2026-11-13-shaliach-punches-the-clock-punch-clock-1536.webp)

**And then I asked for it to happen next month too**

Once it worked, I wanted to turn the one-time fix into something permanent:

> Create a skill and a permanent routine for filling in missing hours on the last workday of every month. On days with no report, fill in hours randomly, so that the total hours each day is at least 8:30 hours.

A skill is a capability you can run again and again,
and a routine is a schedule that runs it at the time we set.
Two days later, I updated the minimum to nine hours a day.

Today, on the last workday of the month, at around 15:30,
the shaliach fills in empty days as work from home, at least nine hours a day,
without overwriting existing attendance reports.
Special days, like election day or a holiday, stay for me to review.

![Seffy, relaxed next to a cup of coffee, beside a big calendar whose squares are filling up with checkmarks, one more tile sliding into place.](/images/posts/2026-11-13-shaliach-punches-the-clock-calendar-fills-1536.webp)

Sometimes I need to take a quick look and clarify what to fill in and what to leave empty.
He does the rest of the work himself, including asking me how much I worked each day.
For now, he fills in random hours, starting from 9:00.
Later on, he'll be able to take it from my activity too, for example from the hours when I sent emails.

Instead of waiting for the email from HR and then rushing to fill everything in,
there's now a routine that takes care of it before the month closes.

**A small task, one less thing on my mind**

It's easy to brush these things off.
Filling in hours, really? You need a shaliach for that?

But this is exactly the kind of task that comes back every month, gets pushed off again and again,
and in the end keeps both me and whoever has to chase me busy.

Not every use of AI has to start with a big project.
Sometimes it's enough to take something small that bugs you regularly, and make sure someone handles it.

And maybe next time my mom talks about HR,
I'll be able to listen without being the living example of the problem.
