I wanted to discover new music. So I sent a shaliach.
(Shluchim, singular shaliach, is Hebrew for "emissaries". It's what I call my AI agents.)

I want to hear new music.
Widen my taste a bit, find things I don't know,
also outside the mainstream.

But in practice, I never get to it.


Going through blogs, reading Reddit threads,
checking what people are excited about,
and then finding the songs on Spotify.

There's a whole world of music out there.
And I want someone to do that work for me.
I just don't get to it, and honestly I don't have the energy.
I mostly want good songs, without all the digging.

![Seffy next to a quiet turntable, no energy to dig through blogs](/images/posts/music-discovery-playlist-01-seffy-overwhelmed-2048.webp)

So I asked my shaliach.

This is the original prompt, as it was, including the dictation mess:

> I want to open up my musical taste and be more into music discovery. I'd love to get something regularly, once a week you go through all the interesting places on Reddit and places like that where people talk about music, the music blogs and so on, a few different areas, and you make me a playlist for that week on Spotify and send it to me, so I can listen to interesting music from Sunday to the next Sunday.

He asked which artists and genres I like,
so he could aim the picks.

I answered:

> I don't have anything specific. Want to broaden my horizons but not mainstream

That was basically the whole request.
Leave room for surprise.


## What he does every week:

We set a routine (a fixed task the shaliach runs on his own) for Sunday morning.

He goes through music discussions and blogs,
collects about 20 songs from a few directions,
and builds a playlist on Spotify.

Next to some of the picks he adds a short note,
and a link to where he found them.

Older music gets in too.

![Seffy by a vinyl stack and a computer, this week's picks arriving](/images/posts/music-discovery-playlist-03-weekly-collect-2048.webp)


## What did I get this week?

**[Odu Gol Golesa by Teodros Makonnen](https://open.spotify.com/track/1eLg1sv3zetWJIL4lWQXDd)**

Ethiopian melodies with a synthesizer and a drum machine.
The tip came from [a Bandcamp Daily piece](https://daily.bandcamp.com/features/teodros-makonnen-with-his-organ-feature) (Bandcamp's magazine, a site where artists sell music directly),
about Makonnen and his recordings from the early eighties.

Together with the song, you also get a bit of the story behind it.

**[Carottes by 1tbsp and miki](https://open.spotify.com/track/1QFuthXFCXJtloV6Vq7uke)**

Here the direction is electronic.
According to [the Stereogum piece](https://stereogum.com/2516630/1tbsp-big-soaps-album-announce-carottes-miki/music) (a music news site),
the song started from an audio clip that miki collected and edited,
and 1tbsp cut and shaped into the main chords.

That's another way to meet a song:
through a small detail about how it was made.

**[Parliaments And White Roses by Lawn](https://open.spotify.com/track/1m0UufcWyIJZEhe3KUQbfT)**

This pick came from [a daily thread on indieheads](https://www.reddit.com/r/indieheads/comments/1xl3ryy/sunday_daily_music_discussion_01_november_2026/) (an indie music community on Reddit).
Someone wrote that the band had a new song out, different from their other material,
and attached a link.

These are exactly the places I asked him to go through.
People who write about music, share something they found,
or know how to say why it's worth paying attention.


## What a joy. Every week I get music I wouldn't have found any other way:

From browsing places I can't get through myself,
through the picks and the notes,
to a playlist already waiting on Spotify.

Alright, I'm off to listen. I've dug enough.
