I was in a meeting with the DevOps team.
Someone brought up Chartbeat, a tool I hadn't heard of before.
They explained that it's the easiest thing in the world, delivers great real-time monitoring,
thought the pricing should be reasonable, and a senior person at our company was already using it daily and loving it.

Before the meeting was even over,
while still sitting in the chair right across from them,
I fired off a quick message to Basher:
> Listen, reach out to the company and get me a price quote.

(Shluchim, singular shaliach, is Hebrew for "emissaries". It's what I call my AI agents.)

A minute or two later he replied:
I reached out through their site. I'll track it and update you the second they respond.

When they got back by email, they dropped a typical enterprise sales questionnaire:
four in-depth questions about our traffic volumes and business goals,
and most importantly: monthly pageview volume.

Anyone who has worked with software vendors knows this trap:
if you throw out a rough guess off the top of your head, you're taking a risk.
Guess too high, and the pricing tier jumps and doubles your bill.
Guess too low, and they won't treat you like a serious lead.

And in my busy daily schedule, I don't have a spare hour to log into GA4 (Google Analytics 4),
build custom segments, calculate 30-day averages, and draft responses in corporate English.
I wanted a precise reply backed by real numbers from the field,
something I could simply paste and send without breaking a sweat.


## How it worked:

Basher and the analytics shaliach wrapped up the entire job that very morning:

**1. Pulling verified numbers from the system.**
They pulled our exact traffic data directly from the site's official GA4 property:
pageview volumes, session trends, and precise averages over the trailing 30 days.
Everything backed by real data, without guesswork or cutting corners.

**2. A full, professional response.**
Instead of just tossing back a dry figure, the shaliach drafted a comprehensive response addressing all four questions:
what exactly we need in real time, who on the team will evaluate the tool,
and an honest breakdown of what works and what we're currently missing in GA4 and Clarity (a tool that records user sessions on the site).

**3. Copy, paste, and send.**
The entire package came back drafted in natural, professional English.
I copied it, hit reply,
and two days later I was already sitting on a Zoom call with them.

## What came out:

From a casual mention in a meeting to a Zoom call with the vendor in just 48 hours,
without me having to dig through analytics reports, calculate averages, or draft emails.


## What to take from this:

**Take things off your plate in real time.**
Hear a good idea or an interesting tool in a meeting?
Don't save it on a to-do list for two weeks from now.
One sentence to your shaliach while still in your chair, and the gears are already turning.

**Don't guess numbers in front of vendors.**
Pricing tiers cost real money.
Let your analytics shaliach pull verified data straight from the source.

**A complete reply fast-tracks the meeting.**
When you return precise, professional answers that same morning,
you skip two weeks of pointless back-and-forth and jump straight to business.

P.S.
It's crazy expensive, but a super cool tool. With our kind of traffic, it just doesn't make financial sense.
